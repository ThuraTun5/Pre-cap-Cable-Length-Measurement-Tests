Motor commands from the Pi

After this page you can explain what S, P, R and X do, which state the motors are in, why Reset never twists the cable, and why the motors stop when the link is silent.

Short page. Details for the Pi programmer are in docs/PROTOCOL.md (v2).

Section 1 · the hard ideaThe motor states

The program is a state machine: the motors are always in exactly one state, and a command moves them to another. Every change is reported to the Pi at once.

In one sentence: S runs, P stops and holds, S continues, X goes all the way back to the start.

Section 2Why Reset unwinds every turn

The program counts every step since Start. Reset tells both motors "go to step 0", so they turn back exactly as many turns as they made, in the opposite direction. The shortest way back would leave the cable rotated by whole turns.

Reverse doesn't confuse this: turning backwards simply counts down. If you made 5 turns forward and 2 back, Reset turns back 3.

Section 3The link watchdog

A watchdog is a timer that someone must keep "feeding". Here the Pi feeds it: any line it sends (at least the tiny keep-alive .) restarts a 2-second timer. If the timer runs out while the motors turn, the ESP32 stops them and reports a fault.

Why: the Pi's screen is the only way to control the motors. If the Pi program crashes or the cable comes loose, nobody could press Stop. The motors then stop by themselves and keep holding, so Reset still works afterwards.

In one sentence: no message from the Pi for 2 s means the motors stop by themselves.
