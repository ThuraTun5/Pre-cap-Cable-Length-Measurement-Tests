Encoder → mm

After this page you can turn encoder counts into slider travel and cable length, explain why we calibrate, and use the z / c / o / d commands.

Short page on purpose. The only important formula is in section 1.

OverviewThe chain from belt to cable length

Section 1Counts → mm

The GT2 belt has a tooth every 2 mm. The pulley has 20 teeth, so one turn pulls 40 mm of belt. One turn is 8192 counts (T4).

\[ \text{mm per count} = \frac{20 \times 2\ \text{mm}}{8192} = 0.00488\ \text{mm} \qquad \text{travel} = \text{count} \times \text{mm per count} \]

So 1000 mm of travel is about 25 turns, or 204 800 counts. One count is about 5 µm, far finer than the 1 mm target.

In one sentence: travel = count × 40 / 8192 mm, before calibration.

Section 2 · the hard ideaWhy we calibrate, and how

Why this matters: 40 mm per turn is the ideal value. The real belt sits a little deeper or higher in the pulley teeth, and belts stretch a little. A 0.4 % difference already gives a 4 mm error over 1000 mm.

Calibration measures the real value: you move the slider an exactly known distance D, and the ESP32 counts how many counts N that took:

\[ \text{mm per count} = \frac{D}{N} \]

In the program: c at the start mark, move exactly D, then c D (for example c 900). The new value is saved. As a safety check, the program refuses a result that differs more than 10 % from 40 mm/turn: that would mean a typo or a slipping belt.

Section 3Zero, offset and cable length

The encoder only counts movement. After power-on it doesn't know where the slider is. So each time:

  1. Push the slider against its end stop and type z: travel = 0.
  2. The offset is the cable length when the slider is at the end stop (clamp to clamp). You measure it once and type o 123.4.

\[ \text{cable length} = \text{offset} + \text{travel} \]

d flips the direction, so that pulling the slider away makes the length bigger.

In one sentence: end stop + z sets zero; length = offset + travel.

Section 4Saved settings

The calibration, offset and direction are stored in the ESP32's flash memory with the Preferences library (Espressif's NVS). They stay after power-off, so you calibrate once, not every day. The zero is not saved, on purpose: the slider may have moved while the power was off.

prefs.begin("t05", false);                               // open the storage area
mmPerCount = prefs.getDouble("mmpc", NOMINAL_MM_PER_COUNT); // read, or use the default
...
prefs.putDouble("mmpc", mmPerCount);                     // save after calibration

In one sentence: calibration is saved in flash; zero is set fresh each time.

PracticeWorked example

Problem. On the real belt you type c, slide exactly 398.4 mm (checked with a reference part), and the ESP32 counted 81 920 counts. (a) What is the true mm per turn? (b) How big would the error be at 1000 mm without calibration?

a

Why: mm per count = D / N; one turn is 8192 counts.

\( \dfrac{398.4}{81\,920} \times 8192 = 39.84\ \text{mm per turn} \) (0.4 % less than 40)

b

Why: the uncalibrated program assumes 40 mm per turn, which is 0.4 % too much.

\( 1000 \times \left(\dfrac{40}{39.84} - 1\right) \approx 4.0\ \text{mm} \) too long. That's 4 times the allowed 1 mm.

Sanity check. 81 920 counts = 10 turns. 10 × 39.84 = 398.4 mm ✓.

Watch outTraps

  • Forgetting z after power-on. The travel then starts from wherever the slider was.
  • Calibrating over a short distance. A 0.5 mm reading error over 50 mm is 1 %; over 900 mm it's 0.06 %.
  • Typing the wrong distance. The 10 % check catches big typos, not small ones. Type carefully.
  • Belt slipping on the pulley. Counts are lost and the calibration is wrong. The belt must be tight.
  • Direction wrong. Length gets smaller when you pull out: type d once.
